# Forms & Booking Experience
Inquiry forms should feel as polished as the portfolio. Ask only for information that is useful for the next conversation. Prefer concise fields such as name, email, service/session type, preferred date or timeframe, and a short message when appropriate. Do not imply that a form sends data unless submission behavior exists.

Style labels, inputs, focus, validation and success/error states deliberately. On a static site with no backend, make the limitation explicit or use a truthful mail/contact action rather than pretending a POST will be delivered.

Master standard: the form is visually integrated, accessible, low-friction, and honest about what happens after submission.
